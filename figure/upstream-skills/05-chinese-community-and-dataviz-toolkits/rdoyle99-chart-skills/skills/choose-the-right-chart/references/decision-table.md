# Decision table

| Question the viewer asks | Data shape | Use | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| What share is each part? | 2 to 6 parts that add to 100% | Pie | Angle shows share at a glance |
| What share, with a total to show? | same | Doughnut | The hole holds the total or a label |
| Which is biggest, and by how much? | any number of categories | Bar, sorted | Length is easier to compare than angle |
| How did it change? | values over time | Line | Slope shows direction |
| Two groups across categories | 2 to 4 series | Grouped bar | Side-by-side comparison |
| Parts of a whole over time | 3 to 5 parts, 4+ periods | Stacked bar | Keeps both total and mix |

## Slice limits
- 2 to 5 slices: pie is fine.
- 6 to 8 slices: pie only if the top two or three slices carry the story.
- 9 or more: group the tail into Other or use a sorted bar.
- Other should stay smaller than the smallest named slice you want the viewer to read. If Other is the biggest slice, the categories are wrong.
- Slices under 3% are unreadable as pie slices; fold them into Other or label them outside.
