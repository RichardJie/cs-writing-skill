#### Verbatim Slide & Figure Accessibility Rules from Okabe & Ito (<https://jfly.uni-koeln.de/color/>)

- **Epidemiology & Peer-Review Probability**:
  > *"One in twelve Caucasian (8%), one in 20 Asian (5%), and one in 25 African (4%) males are so-called 'red-green' colorblind... There should be more than TEN colorblinds in a room with 250 people... Supposing that your paper will be reviewed by three white males... the probability that at least one of them is colorblind is whopping 22%! [ 22% = 1-(1-0.92)^3 ]"*
- **3(+1) Principles of Color Universal Design**:
  1. *"Choose color schemes that can be easily identified by people with all types of color vision, in consideration with the actual lighting conditions and usage environment."*
  2. *"Use not only different colors but also a combination of different shapes, positions, line types and coloring patterns, to ensure that information is conveyed to all users including those who cannot distinguish differences in color."*
  3. *"Clearly state color names where users are expected to use color names in communication."*
  4. *(+1)* *"Moreover, aim for visually friendly and beautiful designs."*
- **Practical Slide, Plot, Micrograph & Oral Delivery Rules**:
  - **Fluorescence & Heatmaps**: *"In the fluorescent double-staining micrographs, DNA chips, etc, do not use the combination of red and green. Use magenta (purple) and green instead... The region of double positive becomes white, which is easily distinguishable for colorblinds."*
  - **Direct Labeling Over Separate Legends**: *"For graphs and line drawings, label elements of the graph on the graph itself rather than making a separate color-coded key, since matching same colors in distant places is extremely difficult."*
  - **Redundant Coding**: *"Do not convey information in color only. Show difference BOTH in color and shape (solid and dotted lines, different symbols, various hatching, etc.)."*
  - **Stroke Thickness & Bold Sans-Serif for Colored Text**: *"When the size of color-coded objects is small, only a few cone cells can be used for recognition. Colorblind people find it especially difficult to distinguish the colors of thin lines and small symbols. For color-coded text, use bold fonts such as Arial or Helvetica, rather than thin Times or NewYork."*
  - **Never Use Dark Red on Dark/Black Slide Backgrounds**: *"Especially for protanopes, who cannot detect long wavelength of red light, dark red appears almost as black. Thus, avoid using red characters on black backgrounds, including blackboards. Note that black text and red text look the same for protanopes."*
  - **Oral Presentation Speech & Laser Pointer**: *"When explaining figures, avoid using only color names and describe also shapes or positions. For example, avoid saying 'this red cell'. Instead, say 'this red, round cells on the top left'"*; *"Since red laser pointers use long wavelength of light, it is often difficult for colorblinds to see where is pointed... green laser pointers becomes available, whose color is easy to see both for colorblinds and nom-colorblinds."*

## 3. Western Presentation Typography, Figure Adaptation & Cognitive Layout Standards

### 3.1 Matthew Butterick — *Practical Typography: Presentations* & *Summary of Key Rules*

- **Author**: Matthew Butterick
- **Verified URLs**:
  - Presentations chapter: <https://practicaltypography.com/presentations.html>
  - Summary of key rules: <https://practicaltypography.com/summary-of-key-rules.html>

#### Verbatim Principles & Excerpts from *Presentations* (<https://practicaltypography.com/presentations.html>)

1. **Physiological Viewing Conditions (Darkened Auditorium vs. Lit Laptop)**:
   > *"Because your readers—i.e., the people in the audience—will be viewing your slides under completely different lighting conditions than you, while you’re writing them... if your audience is in a dimmed room, their pupils will be dilated, and their eyes more sensitive to bright light. So when those white slides are projected at epic scale, they’ll just become a series of GIANT WHITE RECTANGLES floating in the air for forty minutes. Staring at GIANT WHITE RECTANGLES for that long is painful. It will cost you reader attention."*
2. **Darkened-Room Background & Contrast Calibration**:
   > *"Then, in your presentation program, start with a black background. Not dark gray, nor a dark gradient—just black. You want to get the words on screen using the fewest photons possible. So try a thin sans serif font... As for font color, pure white type on a black background can have an uncomfortable degree of contrast. So start your font at 50% gray and brighten it from there. When it’s legible, stop. The thinner the font is, the brighter it will need to be."*
3. **Ordinary-Lighting Background & Simultaneous Contrast (Avoiding Pure `#000000` on `#FFFFFF`)**:
   > *"If your presentation won’t be given in a darkened room, the text-color problem is less urgent. Still, you should avoid putting pure black type on a pure white background. Making the background light gray, or the type dark gray, will reduce contrast and improve reading comfort... when projected under ordinary lighting, that pale gray will read as white. This is an optical phenomenon known as simultaneous contrast."*
4. **Disable Auto-Fit Text Resizing & Lock Consistent Base Point Size**:
   > *"Often, presentation programs will automatically resize the text to fit the slide. Turn this feature off. Better to change your text to suit the point size than vice versa."*
   > *"Pick a base point size that lets you fit 12–15 lines of text on screen. Not that you’ll ever be putting that much on a slide. (I hope.) But that’s a comfortable reading size. As much as possible, use this same point size for every slide—even if there’s only one line of text on screen. Constantly changing the point size between slides is annoying."*
5. **Color Restraint, Left Alignment, Bullet Elimination & Designing Outward on Wide 16:9 Screens**:
   > *"In particular, use color with restraint—prefer pale shades over bright ones. And avoid centered text."*
   > *"Do you really need list bullets? Maybe not. In body text, bullets make the start of each list item visually distinct from the start of a new line or paragraph. But in a presentation, if all your powerful points are less than one line, you can omit the bullets."*
   > *"Recall from maxims of page layout that it’s wise to design outward from the text, not inward from the page edges. This is relevant to presentations, as the wide aspect ratios of computer screens are inconvenient for typography (and especially those of HD-style displays). Feel free to confine your presentation layout to less than the full width of the screen. No one will be bothered that you didn’t fill the whole space. But be consistent across all your slides."*

#### Core Quantitative Rules from *Summary of Key Rules* (<https://practicaltypography.com/summary-of-key-rules.html>)

- **Line spacing**: *"Line spacing should be 120–145% of the point size."*
- **Line length**: *"The average line length should be 45–90 characters (including spaces)."*
- **Emphasis**: *"Use bold or italic as little as possible, and not together."* / *"Never underline, except perhaps for web links."*
- **All caps & letterspacing**: *"All caps are fine for less than one line of text."* / *"Use 5–12% extra letterspacing with all caps and small caps."* / *"Kerning should always be turned on."*
- **Paragraph separation**: *"Use first-line indents that are one to four times the point size of the text, or use 4–10 points of space between paragraphs. Don’t use both."*

### 3.2 Nicolas P. Rougier, Michael Droettboom & Philip E. Bourne (2014) — *Ten Simple Rules for Better Figures*

- **Citation**: Rougier NP, Droettboom M, Bourne PE (2014). *"Ten Simple Rules for Better Figures."* *PLOS Computational Biology* **10**(9): e1003833. `doi:10.1371/journal.pcbi.1003833` (`CC0` Public Domain)
- **Verified URLs**:
  - Article: <https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1003833> (<https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1003833>)
  - Upstream Code Repository (`../upstream-skills/05-disciplines-and-palettes/ten-rules/`, SHA `75ecbdc2ce197564ed77c21a4492ae89ccab0ca8`): <https://github.com/rougier/ten-rules>

#### All 10 Rules & Verbatim Oral-Slide Figure Adaptation Rules

1. **Rule 1: Know Your Audience**
2. **Rule 2: Identify Your Message** — *"A figure is meant to express an idea or introduce some facts or a result that would be too long (or nearly impossible) to explain only with words, be it for an article or during a time-limited oral presentation."*
3. **Rule 3: Adapt the Figure to the Support Medium (Oral Projection vs. Journal Print)**:
   > *"During an oral presentation, a figure will be displayed for a limited time. Thus, the viewer must quickly understand what is displayed and what it represents while still listening to your explanation. In such a situation, the figure must be kept simple and the message must be visually salient in order to grab attention... during oral presentations, figures will be video-projected and will be seen from a distance, and figure elements must consequently be made thicker (lines) or bigger (points, text), colors should have strong contrast, and vertical text should be avoided, etc... Ideally, each type of support medium requires a different figure, and you should abandon the practice of extracting a figure from your article to be put, as is, in your oral presentation."*
   - **Figure 3 Oral Adaptation Adjustments**: *"Many details have been removed (reduced number of trajectories, no overlapping trajectories, reduced number of ticks, bigger axis and tick labels, no title, thicker lines)... some parts have been modified to make them easier to reference (e.g., the yellow box, the red dashed line)."*
4. **Rule 4: Captions Are Not Optional** — If exact numeric values on bar plots matter, provide them explicitly rather than forcing viewers to guess bar heights.
5. **Rule 5: Do Not Trust the Defaults** — Default software styles (*"Matlab, Excel, Keynote, etc."*, *"LaTeX, matplotlib, gnuplot, etc."*) *"are good enough for any plot but they are best for none."*
6. **Rule 6: Use Color Effectively** — *"You need to ask yourself, 'Is there any reason this plot is blue and not black?' If you don't know the answer, just keep it black... Do not use the default colormap (e.g., jet or rainbow) unless there is an explicit reason to do so."* Match colormap class strictly to data structure: **Sequential** (low-to-high quantitative), **Diverging** (deviation from a median), **Qualitative** (discrete/categorical).
7. **Rule 7: Do Not Mislead the Reader** — Encode scalar quantities by disc **area** rather than **radius**; include full y-axis baseline/range on bar plots; avoid 3D and pie charts for quantity comparisons.
8. **Rule 8: Avoid "Chartjunk"** — Quoting Edward Tufte (1983): *"Regardless of the cause, it is all non-data-ink or redundant data-ink, and it is often chartjunk."* Eliminate gratuitous background fills, overlapping legends, 3-decimal tick clutter, and unaligned grids; replace multi-series spaghetti plots with directly labeled small multiples.
9. **Rule 9: Message Trumps Beauty**
10. **Rule 10: Get the Right Tool** (`Matplotlib`, `R`, `Inkscape`, `TikZ/PGF`, `GIMP`, `ImageMagick`, `D3.js`, `Cytoscape`, `Circos`).

- **Citations & Verified URLs**:

- **Documented Principles**:
  - **Low Spatial Resolution of Slides**: Projected slides have inherently low information resolution compared with printed technical tables/reports; presenters must not truncate complex causal evidence into slogan-like fragments.

### 4.1 W3C `clreq` — *Requirements for Chinese Text Layout (中文排版需求)*

- **Editors**: 薛富侨 (Fuqiao XUE, W3C) & Richard Ishida (W3C), Chinese Layout Task Force / W3C Internationalization Working Group
- **Verified URLs**:
  - Canonical W3C Technical Report: <https://www.w3.org/TR/clreq/>
  - Upstream GitHub Repository: <https://github.com/w3c/clreq> (HEAD SHA verified: `08a701fafc77645afcc83c5dc662abb2455ae825`)

#### Verbatim W3C `clreq` Rules Relevant to Chinese & Bilingual Academic Slides

1. **§4.1 Characters, Proportional Western Fonts & STEM Formula Punctuation**:
   - Avoid legacy fullwidth ASCII characters (`U+FF01`–`U+FF5E`): *"过去，由于计算机排版技术不精良，多使用‘全角ASCII字符’以达到整齐等视觉排版效果。现今在文本储存时，应避免使用该区段的拉丁字母及数字字符，交由排版引擎处理比例字体、等宽字体等显示需求。"*
   - STEM formula punctuation exception: *"中西混排中，由于正文是中文，原则上应该使用中文标点，遵守中文标点的习惯用法。但是，涉及公式较多的科学技术中文图书，句号可以统一使用西文句号 U+002E FULL STOP [.]，省略号可以使用西文省略号 U+2026 HORIZONTAL ELLIPSIS […]，字面分布按西文习惯。"*
2. **§6.1.1 Line-Start & Line-End Prohibition Rules (行首行尾禁则, referencing `GB/T 15834—2011`)**:
   - **Recommended `basic` (基本处理) rule**: *"点号（顿号、逗号、句号、冒号、分号、叹号、问号）、结束引号、结束括号、结束单双书名号（书名号乙式）、连接号、间隔号、分隔号不能出现在一行的开头。开始引号、开始括号、开始单双书名号（书名号乙式）等符号，不能出现在一行的结尾。这是最推荐的方法。"*
   - **`strict` (严格处理) rule**: Adds that solidi (`/`), two-em dashes (`——`), and ellipses (`……`) must not appear at line start (`在执行GB法的基础上再增加规定破折号、省略号不能出现在一行的开头`).
3. **§6.1.2 Prohibition Rules for Unbreakable Marks (符号分离禁则)**:
   - Two-em dashes (`——`) and ellipses (`……`) occupy `2 em` and must not wrap across two lines (`占用两个汉字的空间，应视为一体，不能拆成两行`).
   - Numerals and attached units/signs must never split across a line break:
     - *"阿拉伯数字应作为一个整体，不能拆成两行。"*
     - *"百分号（%）、千分号（‰）、度数符号（°、℃、℉）与其前面的阿拉伯数字之间不能拆成两行。"*
     - *"正号（+）、负号（-）、正负号（±）与其后面的阿拉伯数字之间不能拆成两行。"*
     - *"货币符号与其相关的阿拉伯数字之间不能断行。"*
4. **§6.3.2 Punctuation Width Compression (标点符号的宽度调整)**:
   - Adjacent brackets/punctuation: *"如果任意两个相邻标点符号占用2个字宽，应当缩减成1.5个字宽。在此原则上，允许排版风格进一步调整让两个符号只占1个字宽。"*
   - Line-end punctuation trimming (`GB/T 15834—2011` §5.1.10): *"原本占一个字宽的标点出现在行尾时，应缩减该符号末侧二分之一个汉字大小的空白。"*
5. **§6.3.3 Mixed Text Composition in Horizontal Writing Mode (横排的中、西文混排配置)**:
   > *"横排时，西文字母使用比例字体；阿拉伯数字则常用比例字体或等宽字体。原则上，汉字与西文字母、数字间使用不多于四分之一个汉字宽的字距或空白。但西文、数字出现在行首或行尾时，则无须加入空白。在中文点号前后、中文开始夹注符号之后、结束夹注符号之前的西文字母或阿拉伯数字，不调整字距或加入空白。或可使用西文词间空格（U+0020 SPACE [ ]，其宽度随不同字体有所变化）。"*

### 5.3 少数派 × 一周进步（诺灰 / 李自长）— 《那些好看的 PPT 文字排版，都是这样做成的》

- **Authors**: 诺灰 / 李自长（「一周进步」专栏作者，责编 `@文刀漢三`), published `2018-12-23`
- **Verified URL**: <https://sspai.com/post/51188>
- **Verbatim PPT Typography & "去Word化" Rules**:
  - **Root Cause of Slide Reading Obstacles**: *"我们遇到的 PPT 让人不舒服的 90% 情况，都可以归结成一句话，就是 版面中主体与层级关系不明确"* — manifested as insufficient whitespace, over-stacked emphasis (combining bold + color + size + italic + underline + shapes simultaneously), unaligned elements, and cramped line/group spacing.
  - **Hierarchy Cap (`1–3` Levels)**: *"平面设计中文字的层级在要求在 2-6 个之间，而 PPT 作为一种需要在短时间内传达信息的平面设计手段，我们要 将文字的层级数目控制在 1-3 个之间。对于文字层级的划分，我们按照 先父子级、再兄弟级 的方式进行划分。"*
  - **Spacing Inequality Law (CRAP Proximity)**:
    - *"文字组与文字组之间的距离要明显大于文字组内部的行间距；"*
    - *"文字组内的行间距和组与组之间的距离，一定要大于文字的字间距；"*
    - **Line Spacing**: *"行间距控制在 1.2-1.4 倍之间（多倍行距 1.2–1.4）。"*
    - **Display Title Tracking (`> 24 pt`)** (citing 研习设 K先生): *"对于 24 号以上的标题文字，字间距切忌使用加宽等方式。原因是字库中的字体默认设置，是以文字在小字号下排版效果而专门调整的，默认间距较为透气，当文字放大后，字体之间的距离会变得松散，为了提高阅读性，需要缩小文字之间的距离，形成一个区域面积。"*
  - **Word-Paste Refactoring ("去Word化" 3-Step Workflow)**: When converting dense text pasted from Word onto a slide: **(1)** Divide text into logical parent/sibling levels → **(2)** Establish visual hierarchy via CRAP (Contrast, Repetition, Alignment, Proximity) within a defined type area (`确定版心`) → **(3)** Add minimal structural lines/containers only after hierarchy is clear.

### 5.5 `GordenSun/GordenPPTSkill` — 《模式 C：完全原创 PPT 设计原则》

- **Author**: `GordenSun`
- **Verified URL**: <https://github.com/GordenSun/GordenPPTSkill/blob/main/references/original-design-guide.md>
- **Verbatim Minimalist Slide Constraints**:
  - **Anti-Decoration Rules**: *"不要堆砌装饰：避免边框、阴影、渐变、纹理、icon 集群；不要用超过 1 个主色 + 1 个强调色；不要把超过 4 个信息点塞一页；不要用奇怪的字体（华文琥珀、隶书、楷体等装饰字体）；不要做层级嵌套的‘卡片里套卡片’。"*
  - **Margins, Palette, Font Pairing & Point-Size Gradient**:
    - Safe canvas margin: **elements `≥ 1.5 cm` from slide edges** on `16:9` (`33.87 cm × 19.05 cm`) or `4:3`.
    - Max content per slide: `1` title + `1` subtitle + `3–4` core items.
    - Restrained primary colors: `#1F3A68`, `#2C3E50`, or `#B22222` on pure white `#FFFFFF` (with secondary text `#666666`).
    - Font stack: Chinese `微软雅黑` / `思源黑体 CN`; English `Inter` / `Helvetica` / `Arial` (*"标题加粗，正文常规，不用斜体"*).
    - Point-size gradient: **`48–54 pt`** (cover) / **`32–40 pt`** (section title) / **`18–22 pt`** (body) / **`12–14 pt`** (footnote).
