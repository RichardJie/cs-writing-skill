# 04 — Camera Motion Vocabulary

For image-to-video and text-to-video models (Seedance, Veo, Kling, Sora), motion description is half your prompt. This doc gives you 12 precise terms instead of "the camera moves nicely."

---

## The 12 essential motion terms

### 1. Push-in (dolly in)

Camera moves physically toward the subject. Subject grows, background also slightly grows.

```
slow steady push-in toward subject, 6-second duration,
camera glides forward at constant speed,
subject remains centered, slight emotional intensification
```

Use for: Building tension, revealing detail, emotional intimacy.

---

### 2. Pull-back (dolly out)

Camera moves away from subject. Reveals context, makes subject feel smaller.

```
slow pull-back reveal, camera retreats from subject,
foreground stays sharp, background context expands into frame,
emphasizes scale and isolation
```

Use for: Reveals, scale, ending shots, loneliness.

---

### 3. Tracking shot (lateral dolly)

Camera moves sideways parallel to the action, keeping the subject in frame.

```
lateral tracking shot, camera glides left-to-right alongside subject,
subject stays centered as background passes,
parallax depth between foreground and background
```

Use for: Walking subjects, action sequences, energy.

---

### 4. Crane up / down

Camera rises or falls vertically while staying focused on subject.

```
slow crane up from low angle to overhead,
subject stays in frame as perspective tilts,
reveals new context above or below
```

Use for: Revealing scale, transition between sections, emotional rises.

---

### 5. Pan

Camera body stays fixed, lens rotates horizontally.

```
slow pan left-to-right across landscape,
camera body locked, lens rotation only,
parallax minimal, like turning your head
```

Use for: Surveying landscape, following lateral movement, scanning.

---

### 6. Tilt

Camera body stays fixed, lens rotates vertically.

```
slow tilt up from feet to face,
camera locked, lens rotation only,
no horizontal shift
```

Use for: Character intro, revealing height/scale, vertical surprise.

---

### 7. Orbit (circle around)

Camera circles around a fixed subject.

```
smooth orbit around subject, 180° arc over 5 seconds,
subject remains centered, background rotates around them,
constant distance from camera to subject
```

Use for: Product hero shots, showing 3D-ness, dramatic moments.

---

### 8. Whip pan / snap zoom

Fast, motion-blurred horizontal pan. Or fast zoom in/out.

```
fast whip pan left-to-right in 0.3 seconds,
heavy motion blur during the transition,
sharp before and after, cuts on the blur
```

Use for: Transitions, energy, comedic timing.

---

### 9. Handheld / shaky

Camera held by hand, organic micro-movements.

```
handheld documentary feel, organic shake,
camera follows subject reactively, not anticipating,
slight overshoot when subject changes direction,
no stabilization, raw immediacy
```

Use for: Realism, vérité, action, anxiety.

---

### 10. Steadicam / gimbal float

Smooth floating movement through space, no shake.

```
smooth gimbal float through environment,
camera glides at constant height,
following subject through space with cinematic stability,
no shake, no abrupt direction changes
```

Use for: Music videos, fluid storytelling, dream sequences.

---

### 11. Rack focus

Focus shifts from one subject to another within the same shot.

```
rack focus from foreground subject to background subject,
shallow depth of field, focus pull over 1.5 seconds,
no camera movement, focal point shift only
```

Use for: Drawing attention, revealing relationships, parallel storytelling.

---

### 12. Lock-off (no movement)

Camera does not move. Subject moves within frame.

```
locked-off camera, no movement,
subject moves through static frame,
all motion comes from subject and environment
```

Use for: Tarkovsky-style contemplation, comedy timing, comparison shots.

---

## Pairing motion with mood

| Mood | Motion recipe |
|---|---|
| Tension building | Slow push-in + low angle |
| Reveal / surprise | Pull-back or crane up |
| Energy / action | Tracking or whip pan |
| Intimacy | Push-in to close-up |
| Scale / awe | Crane up + IMAX wide |
| Anxiety | Handheld micro-shake |
| Dream / fantasy | Steadicam float + slow motion |
| Quiet contemplation | Lock-off + long duration |
| Realism / documentary | Handheld + reactive following |

---

## Lens choices that pair with motion

The lens affects how motion reads. Always specify:

| Lens | Motion feel |
|---|---|
| **14-24mm wide** | Push-in feels accelerated, background grows fast |
| **35mm** | Natural human eye, default for tracking |
| **50mm** | Slight compression, intimate push-ins |
| **85mm portrait** | Heavy background compression, rack-focus heaven |
| **100mm+ telephoto** | Compressed, slow-feeling motion, isolation |

### Example prompt

> "Slow steady push-in toward subject over 6 seconds. **Shot on 85mm at f/2.0**, shallow depth of field, background compressed and blurred. Subject's face fills the lower third by the end of the push. Locked smooth dolly, no shake."

---

## Common mistakes

- ❌ "The camera moves" — vague.
- ❌ "Cinematic camera movement" — meaningless.
- ❌ Combining 3 motion types in 5 seconds — too busy.
- ❌ No duration — model picks default (usually too fast).
- ✅ One primary motion. State direction, speed, duration, ending state.
